\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:experiments_results}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:exp_introduction}
This chapter presents the experimental evaluation of the two modeling phases introduced
in Ch.~\ref{ch:theoretical_framework} and~\ref{ch:data_setup}, assessed with the
metrics defined in Ch.~\ref{ch:evaluation_methodology}. Two research questions guide
the analysis:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Phase~1 --- Replication.} Is a CSDI-based diffusion architecture capable
    of learning the distribution and reproducing the stylized facts of financial time series when trained
    under the SDE framework proposed by Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025diffusion}?
    \item \textbf{Phase~2 --- EDM-CSDI.} Does embedding the same architecture within the EDM framework of Karras et al.~\cite{karras2022elucidating}
    and conditioning generation on same-day Open, High, and Low log-returns improve
    generation quality over the Phase~1 baseline?
\end{enumerate}

The chapter is organised in four sequential parts. The former introduce two baseline models GARCH-X and Merton-X, this will function as baseline comparison for parametric models, widely accepted in the industry, fully understood and probabilistically modeled. The second part will cover Phase~1, which concern the replication of the work of Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025diffusion} on unconditional univariate generation of S\&P~500 close log-returns. The third part, will investigate the contribution of the EDM framework when applied to Kim et al. CSDI-based architectured, therefore it will retain unconditional information while introducing preconditioning in training. Lastly, the full EDM model will be presented incorporating conditional information and EDM framework.

Subsequently, others smaller experimentations were executed to compare the models behavior under different sampling procedures.

A key design difference that is worth to keep in mind between Phase~1 and Phase~2 concerns the validation split, as described in Sec.\ref{sec:sliding_window_ch4}. In Phase~1, validation windows are drawn randomly from the full sliding-window pool and are partially overlapping with others. In Phase~2, validation windows are strictly held out. Specifically, 239 windows corresponding to approximately 1.5\% of the overall windows and 10\% of the unique rows.

Moreover, because the way data are processed to build windows, the 'Reference' values vary slightly according to the specific configuration that it is being analyzed: raw reference, \(L=2048\) windows with \texttt{stride}\(=100\), or \(L=512\) windows with \texttt{stride}\(=100\).

Unless stated otherwise, all power-law coefficients, \(\hat{\alpha}\) are computed through Alstott et al.\ \cite{alstott2014powerlaw} python package \texttt{powerlaw} on a subsample of \(300,000\) observations. Similarly, \(D_{KS}\) and \(W_1\) are computed on a subsample of 1M observations randomly selected using a fixed seed. Finally, excess kurtosis (Ex. Kurt.) is reported relative to the Gaussian.

The statistics above show consistency in performance and have the advantage that they are bound in kurtosis and skewness. The other statistics, as it is possible to appreciate also by Tab.\ \ref{tab:phase1_raw_with_reference}, match fairly well the distributions in aggregate statistics, nevertheless they still fails in learning the specific patter observable between \(q_{0.02}-q_{0.98}\). The 'Ex. Kurt.' of 'VP + Linear' is likely bound to a poorly learned score field for few samples and ODE truncation error.

Importantly, the interpretation of the log-returns as cumulative didn't yield any useful advantage, instead the learning seemed basic and the generated values are very close to forward added Gaussian Noise, casting doubt on the ability of the model to discern between signal and noise.

Clearly the VE SDE is superior than VP with linear noise scheduler that smooth out the distribution. This is interesting because while in training we observed lower losses for the predicted added noise \(\hat{\epsilon}\) due to  that while more promising in training it has more difficulties in reconstructing the series and it is either unstable or smooth excessively the distribution, implying that more model expressivity might matter more than detailed reconstruction. There are still two limitations distributionally of the VE approach: first it undershoot tail reconstruction, as it is possible to see from Fig.\ \ref{fig:level_marginal_ve_exponantial_2048}'s QQ-plot. Secondly, it misses the 0\% returns, as shown by the generated ECDF cutting through the central vertical jump.

\textbf{Stylized Facts} \quad Our estimation of the \(\alpha\) component is in line with Kim el al., which reported a \(\alpha\approx4.35\), while for us it was estimated at \(4.37\), therefore in line. An estimation below this benchmark indicates that the data has a heavier tails.

The core advantage of Phase~1 models on baseline ones is its ability to recover the stylized facts of the training windows, especially leveraged effect, even if with a lower scale than the reference one.

\subsection{Phase~2 Unconditional EDM-CSDI}

The next phase focused on understanding how the EDM formulation impact the generalization and stylized facts learning of the CSDI models. From here onward we will refer to the current model as \emph{EDM+Unconditional} (Unc.).

In particular an improvement was measured in the ability to capture the decimalization 0\% returns effect. This is likely due to the fact that in Phase~1 the model still tries to predict the added noise, \(\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)\), which makes the precise inference of the missed amount harder, while for the EDM framework which predicts directly the output values it is easier, possibly overestimating because it is an easy cheatcode to reduce errors. Irregardless of such improvement the ability to capture heavy tails seem to remain somewhat limited.

\section{Phase 3 --- EDM-CSDI Sampling exploration}
In this section we will discuss some of the exploration that has been conducted in order to assess if samples improvements are possibles and how does the model behave when stochasticity is re-introduced during the generation phase.

This section will contain three sampling exploration: second order Heun's stochastic sampler, \(\sigma_{\max}\) sampler prior, and NFEs exploration. Specifically, the former is to evaluate if the model can recover some peculiarity of FTS paths through the injection of stochasticity; the second is to explore whether further or closer prior to the original distribution may influence sampling quality while retaining the same computational cost; and as last the NFEs to check if reconstruction error is already under control with current settings \(NFEs=400\) or \(steps=200\), or if there is margin for improvement, and decreasement, given that this was an upper bound.

\textbf{Heun Stochastic Sampler} \quad In the first phase we generated data under the same condition as sub-Sec.\ \ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi}, but using a stochastic sampler. Specifically, we experimented with a budget of \(S_{\text{churn}}=10\), \(S_{\text{tmin}}=0.05\), \(S_{\text{tmax}}=\infty\), and \(S_{\text{noise}}=1.0\). Over \(20,000\) windows the distributional properties were the same.

A necessary condition for attributing Phase~2 improvements to the EDM framework or to
the conditioning signal is a fair comparison with the Phase~1 baseline on identical
windows and identical evaluation splits. To this end, the best Phase~1 configuration
identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:phase1_summary} is retrained from scratch at $L=512$ and
stride $s=100$, using the probability flow ODE sampler and the same validation split
employed in Phase~2. This retrained model is referred to as Phase~1$^*$ throughout
this section. Because the window length, stride, and evaluation split are now identical
for both models, differences in generation quality can be attributed to the modelling
choices rather than to differences in the data seen during training.

The comparison is conducted on the metrics used throughout Phase~1 --- KS statistic,
$W_1$ distance, tail exponent $\alpha$, and pathwise aggregate statistics --- computed
on the generated Close log-return channel in both cases. CRPS is reported for Phase~2 BM
but is not applicable to Phase~1$^*$, which is unconditional and does not produce a
predictive distribution over the Close channel given OHL context. The isolated
contribution of conditioning to generation quality is examined in
