\chapter{Data and Training Setup}
\label{ch:data_setup}

\section{Data}

The dataset follows the source and structure reported by Kim et al.\ \cite{kim2025diffusion}.
Specifically, the list of current S\&P 500 members was taken as the starting universe, and
tickers with non-standard symbols (e.g., BRK.B, BF.B) were excluded. For each remaining
ticker, the maximum available daily price history was downloaded. The dataset was then
filtered to retain only those stocks whose available history extends more than 40 years back.
According to the authors:
\begin{quote}
    \emph{This selection criterion ensures that the dataset encompasses diverse market
    regimes, including various boom and bust cycles, thus providing a rich context for
    training the generative model.}
\end{quote}
For each selected stock, daily log-returns were computed as in Eq.~\ref{eq:log_returns},
using the adjusted closing price on day \(t\).\\

This selection is subject to survivorship bias: only companies that remained listed for a
period substantially longer than the average tenure in the S\&P 500 index are included
\texttt{[REFERENCE]}. The resulting universe therefore over-represents long-lived firms
and may understate the frequency of adverse market events associated with delistings
and failures.

\section{Preprocessing}

In Phase 1, only univariate log-returns are used, as defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:log_returns}.
In Phase 2, where conditioning on all four OHLC channels is required, the Open, High,
and Low prices are each expressed as log-returns relative to the previous closing price:
\[
    \hat{O}_t = \log\!\left(\frac{O_t}{C_{t-1}}\right), \qquad
    \hat{H}_t = \log\!\left(\frac{H_t}{C_{t-1}}\right), \qquad
    \hat{L}_t = \log\!\left(\frac{L_t}{C_{t-1}}\right)
\]
This formulation is consistent with the Close log-return defined in Eq.~\ref{eq:log_returns},
expressing all channels relative to the same reference price \(C_{t-1}\).\\

A per-channel z-score normalisation is then applied independently to each of the four
channels. This is a linear transformation that preserves the shape of each channel's
distribution while standardising it to zero mean and unit variance, so that
\(\sigma_{\text{data}} = 1\) holds by construction and network inputs remain numerically
stable across channels. However, because each channel is scaled by its own standard
deviation, the ordering constraint \(L_t \leq \min(O_t, C_t) \leq \max(O_t, C_t) \leq H_t\),
which holds in price space, is not preserved after normalisation. This is a structural
limitation acknowledged throughout the evaluation.

\section{Sliding Window Construction}

Training sequences are constructed via a sliding window over the full dataset. Two
configurations are used across the two experimental phases:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Phase 1, replication:} window length \(L = 2048\) and stride \(s = 400\),
    yielding approximately 5,500 training windows over the full dataset.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2, EDM-CSDI:} window length \(L = 512\) and stride \(s = 100\),
    consistent with the conditional OHLC setting described in
    Ch.~\ref{ch:theoretical_framework}.
\end{itemize}

A window of length \(L = 2048\) spans approximately ten years of daily returns and is
therefore well-suited to capturing long-term trends. However, the temporal Transformer
incurs an \(\mathcal{O}(L^2)\) cost in sequence length, making it computationally
expensive. The shorter \(L = 512\) setting was adopted for Phase 2 due to hardware
constraints imposed by MIG partitioning of the A100 GPU into 40 GB slices. The CSDI
self-attention computes a score matrix of shape \((B \times K,\, H,\, L,\, L)\), and
PyTorch selects among three SDPA kernels at runtime. Flash SDPA avoids materialising
the full matrix but requires more CUDA cores than the available MIG slices provide.
Memory-Efficient SDPA encounters an internal limit of 65,535 on the
\(\text{batch} \times \text{heads}\) dimension: with \(K = 4\), \(B = 64\), and
\(L = 2048\), this product reaches 524,288. The only remaining fallback, Math SDPA,
materialises the full \(L \times L\) attention matrix, costing approximately 17 GB for
attention alone at \(L = 2048\), which exceeds the 40 GB budget once model parameters
and activations are included. Reducing to \(L = 512\) brings the attention cost within
budget while still covering approximately two years of daily returns; the stride of 100
maintains sufficient window overlap and training set diversity.\\

Overlapping windows introduce correlation between adjacent training samples, violating
the i.i.d.\ batch assumption. This is a standard and accepted approximation in
deep learning on time series, analogous to patch sampling in image models, but it should
be noted as a limitation when interpreting training dynamics.

\section{Training Configuration}

This section discusses the most relevant training configuration and design choices;
full hyperparameter details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:sec:training_config}.\\

The optimiser used throughout this project is \texttt{AdamW}
\cite{loshchilov2019decoupled}. At each step, the first and second moment estimates
of the gradient, \(\hat{m}_t\) and \(\hat{v}_t\), are used to compute a per-parameter
adaptive learning rate, while weight decay is applied directly to the parameters rather
than through the gradient, preventing the interaction between \(\ell_2\) regularisation
and the adaptive scaling that occurs in standard Adam. The parameter update rule is:
\[
    \theta_t = \theta_{t-1}
        - \eta \frac{\hat{m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \varepsilon}
        - \eta \lambda\, \theta_{t-1}
\]
where \(\eta\) is the learning rate, \(\lambda\) is the decoupled weight decay
coefficient, \(\varepsilon\) is a small constant for numerical stability, and
\(\hat{m}_t = m_t / (1 - \beta_1^t)\), \(\hat{v}_t = v_t / (1 - \beta_2^t)\) are the
bias-corrected moment estimates with \(\beta_1, \beta_2\) controlling the exponential
decay of the first and second moments respectively. This decoupling leads to more
consistent regularisation across parameters with different gradient magnitudes, which
improves generalisation in practice.\\

The learning rate is scheduled via cosine annealing \cite{loshchilov2017sgdr}, which
decreases the learning rate progressively according to:
\[
    \eta_t = \eta_{\min} + \frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min})
    \left(1 + \cos\!\left(\frac{t}{T}\pi\right)\right)
\]
where \(\eta_{\max}\) and \(\eta_{\min}\) are the peak and minimum learning rates,
\(t\) is the current training step, and \(T\) is the total number of steps. The
schedule enables larger updates early in training, when the loss landscape is steep,
and progressively smaller refinements towards convergence. A known risk of this
approach is entrapment in a local minimum if the learning rate decays below the
threshold needed to escape it. For the replication phase we set a \(\eta_\text{max}=5\cdot10^{-4}\) and a \(\eta_{\text{min}}=1\cdot10^{-6}\). Experiments were also conducted with
Reduce-on-Plateau, which decreases the learning rate when a
monitored validation metric stagnates; this yielded consistently poorer training
performance than cosine annealing and was therefore not adopted.\\

Both the replication and EDM phases use the same model architecture, with 128
\texttt{channels} and 6 \texttt{layers}. This configuration was selected empirically:
it achieves greater expressivity than the 64 \texttt{channels} and 8 \texttt{layers}
variant, while remaining computationally comparable to the 256 \texttt{channels} and
6 \texttt{layers} variant, which did not yield any measurable improvement in
generation quality.\\

Regarding embedding dimensions, a 256-dimensional embedding is used for both the
diffusion-step and time embeddings, and a 64-dimensional embedding for the feature
axis, following Kim et al.\ \cite{kim2025diffusion}.\\

The values of \(\sigma_{\max}\) and \(\sigma_{\min}\) vary depending on the SDE
formulation and are particularly relevant for the replication phase, as they define
the range of noise injected during the forward process. For VE-style diffusion on
log-returns, \(\sigma_{\max} = 1\) and \(\sigma_{\min} = 0.01\); for GBM-style
diffusion on cumulative log-returns, \(\sigma_{\max} = 10\) and
\(\sigma_{\min} = 0.1\). For the VP formulation, the noise schedule is instead
parameterised by \(\beta_{\max} = 8\) and \(\beta_{\min} = 0.01\).

Two further parameters relevant specifically to Phase 2 are \(P_{\text{mean}}\) and
\(P_{\text{std}}\), which control the log-normal noise level sampling distribution
(Eq.\ref{eq:edm_sigma_sampling}). Their values were determined through ablation over the
grid \(P_{\text{mean}} \in \{-0.8,\,-1.0,\,-1.2,\,-1.4,\,-1.6\}\) and
\(P_{\text{std}} \in \{1.0,\,1.2,\,1.4,\,1.6,\,1.8\}\). The configuration
\(P_{\text{mean}} = -1.4\) and \(P_{\text{std}} = 1.6\) yielded the best training
performance and was adopted for all Phase 2 experiments.
