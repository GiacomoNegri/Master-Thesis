\section{Discussion of Results}
\label{sec:discussion_results}

The results of Ch.~\ref{ch:experiments_results} establish that the EDM-CSDI (Cond.) model substantially outperforms its Phase~1 ancestors and the classical baselines. Beyond this headline finding, several patterns emerge when the results are read \emph{across} phases rather than within them. This section discusses five such cross-cutting observations, each of which motivates one or more of the future directions in Sec.~\ref{sec:future_work}.

\textbf{Prediction Target and the Decimalization Spike} \quad A subtle but informative thread runs through Figs.~\ref{fig:level_marginal_ve_exponantial_2048} and~\ref{fig:density_edm_unconditional}. The Phase~1 models, which predict the injected noise $\epsilon$, produce an ECDF that cuts straight through the central jump caused by zero returns: the $\epsilon$-target carries no information about the discreteness of the underlying price grid, so the network has no incentive to concentrate mass at exactly zero. EDM-CSDI (Unc.), which predicts $x_0$ directly, does the opposite: it \emph{overshoots} the zero-return spike (Fig.~\ref{fig:density_edm_unconditional}). The two parameterizations therefore sit on opposite sides of the same artifact. This is a useful diagnostic: the decimalization spike behaves like a "calibration dial" for how a diffusion model represents near-discrete structure embedded in a continuous variable, and it is a cheap, single-number probe that future configurations can be checked against without recomputing the full battery of distributional statistics.

\textbf{Conditioning as Implicit Volatility-Regime Identification} \quad Every \emph{unconditional} configuration in this thesis underestimates excess kurtosis: VE+Exponential reaches only 14.3--14.4 against a reference of 36.0--39.5 (Tabs.~\ref{tab:phase1_aggregate} and~\ref{tab:ve_phase1_512_100}), and EDM-CSDI (Unc.) reaches 20.0 against 39.5, a 49\% deficit (Tab.~\ref{tab:phase2_edm_unconditional_512_100}). The moment EDM-CSDI is conditioned on same-day OHL, the deficit collapses to 1\% (39.0 vs.\ 39.5, Tab.~\ref{tab:phase2_edm_conditional_512_100}) and $W_1$ improves roughly fivefold. A plausible reading of this pattern is that unconditional generation is implicitly a \emph{mixture} problem: the training corpus pools windows from calm and turbulent regimes, and a model sampling unconditionally must reproduce this mixture without being told, for any given draw, which component it is generating from. If the network cannot reliably infer the regime from noise alone, its samples will be pulled toward an "average" regime, compressing exactly the extreme realizations that drive excess kurtosis. The OHL conditioning variables are strongly correlated with the realized volatility of the day they describe, so they act as a cheap, almost free proxy for the latent regime indicator. Under this view, the gains of Sec.~\ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi} are not only an architectural success of EDM, but also evidence that \emph{tail reproduction in unconditional FTS diffusion is bottlenecked by missing regime information}, not solely by model capacity.

\textbf{Two Asymmetric Failure Modes: Tail Blow-up versus Tail Erosion} \quad GARCH-X and Merton-X overstate excess kurtosis by two to three orders of magnitude (11021.7 and 3736.4 vs.\ 38.5, Tab.~\ref{tab:baseline_aggregate}), driven by a small fraction of paths that escape their working windows. The unconditional diffusion models do the opposite: they systematically \emph{erode} the tails, producing too few extreme observations rather than too many. These are not symmetric errors of the same kind. The parametric models occasionally diverge because their recursive variance dynamics are unbounded; the diffusion models, trained with an $L_2$ denoising objective \cite{vincent2011connection}, are pulled toward the conditional mean of the (corrupted) data at every step, and because extreme observations are rare, their contribution to the average training loss is small. The result is a denoiser that is locally accurate in the bulk but biased toward under-dispersion in the tails -- a known tendency of score-based models on heavy-tailed targets \cite{yu2026diffusion}. Framed this way, the GARCH-X/Merton-X and CSDI failure modes are complementary rather than comparable on a single axis: one family needs \emph{tighter} bounds on rare paths, the other needs \emph{more} of them.

\textbf{Sharper but Not Better Calibrated: an Order-Statistics Explanation} \quad The conditional model reduces MAE from $\approx\!0.62$ (climatology) to $\approx\!0.19$ and reaches a CRPS skill score of 0.7376 (Tab.~\ref{tab:crps_results}), yet remains overconfident at the 95\% and 99\% nominal levels even with 50 samples (Tab.~\ref{tab:coverage_results}). Part of this residual gap may be a statistical artifact of the ensemble size rather than a property of the learned distribution itself. For an empirical interval built from the minimum and maximum of $N$ i.i.d.\ draws, classical order-statistics theory gives an \emph{expected} coverage of approximately $(N-1)/(N+1)$: for $N=25$ this is $24/26\approx92.3\%$, and for $N=50$ it is $49/51\approx96.1\%$. These values are remarkably close to the observed coverage at the 99\% level (93.46\% and 96.89\%, Val split). This suggests that a sizeable share of the apparent overconfidence at extreme quantiles is simply because 25--50 samples cannot, by construction, resolve a 99\% interval -- its endpoints fall outside the range spanned by the available order statistics and must be extrapolated. The genuinely interesting question this raises is how much of the remaining gap (after accounting for this finite-$N$ effect) reflects an actual under-dispersion of the learned conditional density, which only a much larger sampling budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}) could isolate.

\textbf{Champion Selection and the Question of Scale Transfer} \quad The Phase~1 champions carried into Phase~2 (VE+Exponential and VE+Cosine) were selected on $D_{KS}$/$W_1$, the metrics on which they are indeed best (0.037/0.002 and 0.062/0.003, Tab.~\ref{tab:phase1_aggregate}). However, VP+Cosine achieved the tail exponent closest to the reference ($\hat{\alpha}=4.368$ vs.\ $4.378$, an error of $0.010$, against $0.083$ for VE+Exponential) and was not retained. Separately, regenerating the VE champions at $L=512$ produced no systematic improvement over their $L=2048$ behaviour (Tab.~\ref{tab:ve_phase1_512_100}): the kurtosis deficit \emph{persists} across a $4\times$ change in window length. Together these two facts suggest that (i) the selection criterion used to promote models between phases privileges bulk distributional fit over tail behaviour, and (ii) whatever the VE family has learned about the bulk distribution does not obviously depend on, nor transfer cleanly across, the training window length. It remains an open question whether a tail-oriented selection criterion (e.g., $\hat{\alpha}$ or excess kurtosis) would have promoted a different Phase~1 configuration into the EDM framework, and whether that configuration would behave differently under the $L=512$ / conditional setting of Phase~2.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Training Hyperparameter Ablation} \quad No training convergence study was performed, and several EDM hyperparameters were fixed at literature defaults rather than tuned for FTS data. In particular:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $P_{\text{mean}}$ and $P_{\text{std}}$: wider settings (e.g.\ 2.0--2.2) were not tested, and could alter how often high-$\sigma$, high-volatility windows are sampled during training.
    \item $\sigma_{\text{data}}$: fixed to 1 by construction of the normalization; window-wise variation and its interaction with $c_{\text{skip}}$/$c_{\text{out}}$ were not explored.
    \item $\sigma_{\max}$: fixed at a value not derived from the data; Sec.~\ref{sec:future_work} discusses a principled alternative.
    \item $c_{\text{noise}}(\sigma)$: the $1/4$ scaling from Karras et al.\ \cite{karras2022elucidating} was used as-is; alternative scalings for the noise-conditioning embedding were not compared.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Sampler Hyperparameters} \quad Generation used a deterministic second-order Heun sampler with $\rho=7$ (the EDM default). Karras et al.\ note that $\rho=3$ equalizes truncation error across steps, prioritizing fine-grained reconstruction; for FTS, where stylized facts such as volatility clustering depend on mid-range $\sigma$ behaviour, a different $\rho$ could shift sample quality in either direction. The number of function evaluations (NFEs) and the use of stochastic churn ($S_{\text{churn}}>0$) were likewise not explored.

\textbf{Architectural Ablation} \quad Embedding dimensions, the number of residual layers, and the (fixed, sinusoidal) positional encoding were inherited from Kim et al.\ \cite{kim2025diffusion} without ablation. Learnable positional encodings and learnable skip-connection weights were not tested, and could plausibly affect both the bulk-distribution fit discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results} and the model's behaviour across window lengths.

\textbf{Confounded EDM and Conditioning Effects} \quad The hypothesis in Sec.~\ref{sec:ch6_edm_csdi_phase2} that the sharpened zero-return peak of EDM-CSDI (Unc.) follows from the $x_0$-prediction parameterization (rather than from some other side effect of switching frameworks) was not isolated experimentally. A cleaner $2\times2$ design -- CSDI with $x_0$-prediction but without EDM preconditioning, and EDM-CSDI with $\epsilon$-prediction -- would be needed to attribute the spike behaviour, and more generally the gains of Phase~2, separately to (a) the EDM reformulation and (b) the introduction of OHL conditioning. As reported, these two changes were introduced together.

\textbf{Computational Budget for Probabilistic Evaluation} \quad The CRPS and coverage analysis (Tabs.~\ref{tab:crps_results} and~\ref{tab:coverage_results}) used 2{,}000 of the 20{,}000 available windows, with at most 50 samples per window, due to HPC constraints. As discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results}, 25--50 samples are insufficient to resolve 95--99\% intervals without extrapolation, so the true calibration of the model at these levels remains only partially characterized.

\textbf{Scope: Single Asset Universe and Absence of Deep-Learning Baselines} \quad All diffusion models were trained on a single corpus of S\&P~500 close log-returns spanning both the pre- and post-decimalization regimes (Sec.~\ref{sec:ch4_data}) as one undifferentiated dataset. The only baselines considered were classical parametric models (GARCH-X, Merton-X); no comparison was made against other deep generative approaches to financial time series (e.g.\ TimeGAN-style models \cite{yoon2019time} or other diffusion-based generators \cite{vuletic2024fingan,takahashi2025generation}), so the relative standing of EDM-CSDI (Cond.) within the broader deep-learning literature on FTS generation is not established by this thesis.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\textbf{Random Masking} \quad The \texttt{predict\_close} mask used throughout Phase~2 is a fixed channel-wise partition, so the model only ever learns $p(C \mid O,H,L)$. CSDI's \texttt{randmask}, which stochastically varies the conditioning set at each training step, would let the model learn conditional distributions over arbitrary subsets of channels -- e.g.\ predicting the intraday range given only the open, or imputing any missing channel pattern. Whether this added generality helps or hurts the specific \texttt{predict\_close} task, and whether it captures the joint OHLC distribution better, is an open question.

\textbf{Principled $\sigma_{\max}$ Selection and Heavy-Tailed Noising} \quad Rather than ablating $\sigma_{\max}$ by hand over $[5,10]$, the data-driven procedure of Pandey et al.\ \cite{pandey2024heavytailed} -- which estimates $\sigma_{\max}$ by minimizing the mutual information between clean and maximally-noised data -- could remove this choice as a free hyperparameter entirely. More broadly, $t$-EDM (replacing the Gaussian noising distribution with a Student-$t$) preserves the EDM preconditioning structure while changing the tail behaviour of the forward process, and is a natural extension given the systematic tail erosion documented in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results} \cite{yu2026diffusion}.

\textbf{Blended Prediction Targets for the Decimalization Spike} \quad Since $\epsilon$-prediction undershoots and $x_0$-prediction overshoots the zero-return spike (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results}), a prediction target that interpolates between the two as a function of $\sigma$ -- analogous to velocity-style parameterizations used elsewhere in the diffusion literature -- could in principle be tuned to match the spike's empirical magnitude, while retaining the rest of EDM's preconditioning machinery. The decimalization spike's amplitude offers a cheap, single-number target for tuning such a blend.

\textbf{Stochastic EDM Sampling} \quad This thesis used the deterministic Heun ODE sampler ($S_{\text{churn}}=0$) throughout. Karras et al.\ \cite{karras2022elucidating} show that adding stochastic churn -- re-injecting noise and re-denoising during sampling -- can correct accumulated discretization error and improve sample diversity in the image domain. Given that the dominant failure mode identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results} is tail \emph{erosion}, churn is a particularly relevant lever to test: by perturbing trajectories away from the deterministic ODE path, it may allow rare, high-magnitude paths to be reached more often, partially compensating for the mean-seeking bias of the $L_2$ training objective.

\textbf{Constraint-Preserving and Alternative OHLC Conditioning} \quad The current conditioning set (same-day O, H, L) does not enforce the ordering constraint $H \geq C \geq L$ in generated samples; a constraint-preserving generation mechanism (e.g.\ reparameterizing $C$ relative to the $[L,H]$ range) could remove a class of structurally invalid paths. Separately, conditioning on other intraday-derived quantities -- the lower wick or the intraday range, both currently under exploration -- would test whether the CRPS/coverage gains of Sec.~\ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi} are specific to OHL or generalize to other volatility-informative conditioning sets.

\textbf{Regime-Stratified Training and Evaluation} \quad The divergence in leverage-effect intensity between the \emph{Validation} and \emph{Both} splits (Sec.~\ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi}) is consistent with the well-documented fact that the leverage effect strengthens during periods of market stress. Stylized facts in this thesis were evaluated as pooled, time-invariant aggregates; a regime-stratified evaluation (e.g.\ splitting by realized-volatility terciles, or by the pre-/post-2002 decimalization boundary already present in the data) would clarify whether the model's stylized-fact accuracy is uniform across regimes or concentrated in the regimes that dominate the training corpus.

\textbf{Importance Reweighting for Tail Coverage} \quad If tail erosion is partly a consequence of rare extreme windows contributing little to the average training loss (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion_results}), then techniques from the class-imbalance literature for diffusion models -- e.g.\ reweighting the training distribution toward high-volatility windows \cite{qin2023classbalancing} -- offer a direct, architecture-agnostic way to test this hypothesis without changing the noise schedule or prediction target.

Taken together, these directions fall into three groups: those that refine the noising/denoising process itself ($\sigma_{\max}$, heavy-tailed noise, blended targets, stochastic sampling), those that enrich the information available to the model (random masking, alternative conditioning, importance reweighting), and those that sharpen the evaluation protocol (regime-stratified analysis, larger sampling budgets, disentangling EDM from conditioning). The results of Ch.~\ref{ch:experiments_results} suggest that progress on the tail-related limitations of unconditional generation is most likely to come from the second and third groups -- giving the model more of the information it needs to identify the regime it is generating, and evaluating it in a way that does not itself obscure tail behaviour -- while the first group offers the more architecturally novel, but currently more speculative, direction.
