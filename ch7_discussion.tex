\section{Discussion of Results}
\label{sec:discussion_results}

EDM-CSDI (Cond.) show itself as to be the strongest model overall in Ch.\ \ref{ch:experiments_results} and few additional patters surface when reading the results across phases.

\textbf{Prediction Target and the Decimalization Spike} \quad Across Figs.\ \ref{fig:level_marginal_ve_exponantial_2048} and \ref{fig:density_edm_unconditional}. Phase~1 models predict the injected noise \(\epsilon\), and their generated ECDF cuts straight through the central jump at zero returns. This indicates that the \(\epsilon\)-target does not carry any information about the discreteness of the underlying price grid. EDM-CSDI (Unc.), which predicts \(x_0\) directly, does the opposite and slightly overshoots the same spike (Fig.\ \ref{fig:density_edm_unconditional}). The decimalization spike therefore works as a single statistic check of wheter a given target parametrization capture the near-discrete (prices are in cents) hidden in an otherwise continuous log-returns variable.

\textbf{Conditioning, Volatility Regimes, and Two Tail Failure Modes} \quad Every unconditional configuration in this thesis underestimates excess kurtosis: VE+Exponential reaches only 14.3--14.4 against a reference of 36.0--39.5 (Tabs.\ \ref{tab:phase1_aggregate} and \ref{tab:ve_phase1_512_100}), and EDM-CSDI (Unc.) reaches 20.0 against 39.5, a 49\% deficit (Tab.\ \ref{tab:phase2_edm_unconditional_512_100}). Conditioning on same-day OHL closes this deficit (39.0 vs.\ 39.5, Tab.\ \ref{tab:phase2_edm_conditional_512_100}) and improves of roughly five times \(W_1\). A possible reading is that unconditional generation is implicitly a mixture problem: the training pool combine calm and turbulent regimes, and a model sampling unconditionally must reproduce both without being told which one is which. Unless it can be inferred from the noised data alone, samples are pulled towards an average regime. Because OHL are strongly correlated with realized volatility (Ch.\ \ref{app:ch:data_insights}), they provide the missing regime indicator.

On the other hand, GARCH-X and Merton overstate kurtosis by two to three orders of magnitude (11021.7 and 3736.4 vs.\ 38.5, Tab.\ \ref{tab:baseline_aggregate}) due to a small fraction of paths escaping their working windows. The two model families fail in opposite ways: parametric models occasionally diverge, while diffusion models trained without conditioning information are pulled toward the mean and underrepresent rare extremes \cite{vincent2011connection,yu2026diffusion}.

\textbf{Sharper but Not Better Calibrated} \quad The conditional model reduces MAE from \(\approx0.62\) (climatology) to \(\approx0.19\) and reaches a CRPS skill score of 0.7376 (Tab.\ \ref{tab:crps_results}), yet remains overconfident at the 95\% and 99\% nominal levels even with 50 samples (Tab.\ \ref{tab:coverage_results}). Part of this gap may simply be a finite-sample artifact: for an interval built from the min/max of \(N\) i.i.d.\ draws, order-statistics theory gives an expected coverage of \((N-1)/(N+1)\), i.e.\ \(24/26\approx92.3\%\) for \(N=25\) and \(49/51\approx96.1\%\) for \(N=50\) -- close to the observed 93.46\% and 96.89\% at the 99\% level (Val split). With 25--50 samples, a 99\% interval's endpoints fall outside the range of the available order statistics and must be extrapolated. How much of the remaining gap reflects genuine under-dispersion of the learned density, once this finite-\(N\) effect is accounted for, is left open.

\textbf{Champion Selection and Scale Transfer} \quad The Phase~1 champions carried into Phase~2, VE+Exponential and VE+Cosine, were selected on \(D_{KS}\)/\(W_1\), the metrics on which they are indeed best (0.037/0.002 and 0.062/0.003, Tab.\ \ref{tab:phase1_aggregate}). VP+Cosine, however, achieved the tail exponent closest to the reference (\(\hat{\alpha}=4.368\) vs.\ \(4.378\), error \(0.010\), against \(0.083\) for VE+Exponential) and was not retained. Separately, regenerating the VE champions at \(L=512\) produced no systematic improvement over their \(L=2048\) behaviour (Tab.\ \ref{tab:ve_phase1_512_100}): the kurtosis deficit persists across a \(4\times\) change in window length. A tail-oriented selection criterion might therefore have promoted a different configuration into the EDM framework, with unknown consequences for the \(L=512\)/conditional setting.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Training, Sampler, and Architecture Ablation} \quad No training convergence study was performed, and several hyperparameters were fixed at literature defaults rather than tuned for FTS:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \(P_{\text{mean}}\) and \(P_{\text{std}}\): wider settings (e.g.\ 2.0--2.2) were not tested, and could change how often high-volatility windows are sampled during training.
    \item \(\sigma_{\text{data}}\): fixed to 1 by construction; window-wise variation and its interaction with \(c_{\text{skip}}\)/\(c_{\text{out}}\) were not explored.
    \item \(\sigma_{\max}\) was fixed rather than data-derived (Sec.\ \ref{sec:future_work} discusses an alternative), and \(c_{\text{noise}}(\sigma)\) used the \(1/4\) scaling of \cite{karras2022elucidating} as-is.
    \item Sampler: \(\rho=7\) (EDM default) was used throughout, although \(\rho=3\) equalizes truncation error and may suit FTS better given that volatility clustering depends on mid-range \(\sigma\); stochastic churn (\(S_{\text{churn}}>0\)) and the number of NFEs were not varied.
    \item Architecture: embedding dimensions, residual-layer count, and the fixed sinusoidal positional encoding were inherited from \cite{kim2025diffusion} without ablation; learnable positional encodings and skip-connection weights were not tested.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Confounded EDM and Conditioning Effects} \quad The hypothesis that the sharpened zero-return peak of EDM-CSDI (Unc.) follows from \(x_0\)-prediction, rather than some other side effect of the EDM reformulation (Sec.\ \ref{sec:ch6_edm_csdi_phase2}), was not isolated experimentally. A \(2\times2\) design -- CSDI with \(x_0\)-prediction but without EDM preconditioning, and EDM-CSDI with \(\epsilon\)-prediction -- would be needed to attribute the gains of Phase~2 separately to the EDM reformulation and to OHL conditioning, which were introduced together.

\textbf{Computational Budget and Scope} \quad The CRPS and coverage analysis (Tabs.\ \ref{tab:crps_results} and \ref{tab:coverage_results}) used 2{,}000 of the 20{,}000 available windows and at most 50 samples each, due to HPC constraints; as discussed above, this is too few to resolve 95--99\% intervals without extrapolation. More broadly, all diffusion models were trained on a single corpus of S\&P~500 close log-returns spanning both decimalization regimes as one undifferentiated dataset, and the only baselines were classical parametric models (GARCH-X, Merton-X). No comparison was made against other deep generative approaches to FTS \cite{yoon2019time,vuletic2024fingan,takahashi2025generation}, so the standing of EDM-CSDI (Cond.) within the broader deep-learning literature remains unestablished.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\textbf{Random Masking} \quad The \texttt{predict\_close} mask used in Phase~2 is a fixed channel-wise partition, so the model only ever learns \(p(C\mid O,H,L)\). CSDI's \texttt{randmask}, which varies the conditioning set stochastically at each step, would let the model learn conditional distributions over arbitrary channel subsets -- e.g.\ predicting the intraday range from the open, or imputing any missing pattern. Whether this generality helps or hurts \texttt{predict\_close} specifically remains open.

\textbf{Principled \(\sigma_{\max}\) Selection and Heavy-Tailed Noising} \quad Rather than ablating \(\sigma_{\max}\) by hand over \([5,10]\), the procedure of Pandey et al.\ \cite{pandey2024heavytailed}, which estimates \(\sigma_{\max}\) by minimizing the mutual information between clean and maximally-noised data, could remove this hyperparameter entirely. More broadly, \(t\)-EDM -- replacing the Gaussian noising distribution with a Student-\(t\) -- preserves the EDM preconditioning structure while changing the tail behaviour of the forward process, directly targeting the tail erosion documented in Sec.\ \ref{sec:discussion_results} \cite{yu2026diffusion}.

\textbf{Blended Prediction Targets for the Decimalization Spike} \quad Since \(\epsilon\)-prediction undershoots and \(x_0\)-prediction overshoots the zero-return spike (Sec.\ \ref{sec:discussion_results}), a prediction target that interpolates between the two as a function of \(\sigma\) could be tuned to match the spike's empirical magnitude while retaining EDM's preconditioning. The spike's amplitude offers a cheap target for tuning such a blend.

\textbf{Stochastic Sampling and Constraint-Preserving Conditioning} \quad This thesis used the deterministic Heun ODE sampler (\(S_{\text{churn}}=0\)) throughout. Karras et al.\ \cite{karras2022elucidating} show that stochastic churn -- re-injecting noise during sampling -- can correct accumulated discretization error and improve sample diversity; given the tail-erosion failure mode of Sec.\ \ref{sec:discussion_results}, churn is a natural lever for reaching rare, high-magnitude paths more often. Separately, enforcing \(H\geq C\geq L\) in generated samples (e.g.\ by reparameterizing \(C\) relative to \([L,H]\)) would remove a class of structurally invalid paths, and conditioning on the lower wick or intraday range, currently under exploration, would test whether the gains of Sec.\ \ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi} generalize beyond OHL.

\textbf{Regime-Stratified Training, Evaluation, and Reweighting} \quad The divergence in leverage-effect intensity between the \emph{Validation} and \emph{Both} splits (Sec.\ \ref{subsec:conditiona_edm_csdi}) is consistent with the leverage effect strengthening during market stress. Stylized facts here were evaluated as pooled, time-invariant aggregates; a regime-stratified evaluation -- by realized-volatility tercile, or by the pre-/post-2002 decimalization boundary -- would clarify whether accuracy is uniform across regimes or concentrated in those dominating the training corpus. If tail erosion is partly a consequence of rare windows contributing little to the average training loss, reweighting the training distribution toward high-volatility windows, as in class-balancing diffusion \cite{qin2023classbalancing}, offers a direct test of this hypothesis.
